How We Compared These Resources

We rated each resource on five criteria that predict whether it helps you pass:

  • Genuinely free content — how much of the actual CEH material is free versus locked behind a trial, paywall, or "unlock for $X" prompt. A large library that mostly sits behind a paywall scores lower than a smaller fully-free one.
  • No-account / no-credit-card access — can you start studying immediately, or do you have to register and hand over a card before you see value?
  • CEH v13 coverage & explanations — does it map to the current 20-module v13 exam (312-50), and does it explain why an answer is right, not just give you the letter?
  • Hands-on value — free resources that include labs or simulations build the tool skills employers actually test, which pure multiple-choice banks do not.
  • Freshness & authority — is it maintained for v13 (not recycled v12 content), and does the source carry enough domain authority to be cited on its own?

No single resource wins every category — that is why the section at the end shows how to combine two or three free tools into one plan instead of trying to force everything onto one site. The goal is the cheapest path to 80%+ on timed practice, which is the honest booking threshold.

Resource-by-Resource Breakdown

CEHStudy (this site). What is genuinely free: the entire library — 200 original multiple-choice questions with a written explanation on every answer, 306 module-organized flashcards with a local progress tracker, the full v13 study guide, two complete schedules (a 30-day intensive and 6-week plan), 20 module note pages, and a glossary. Open Exam Prep. The clearest strength is question volume plus an AI tutor — 200+ CEH questions and a dedicated flashcards page, with blog articles that capture a lot of long-tail search. The free tier is real, but the premium layer (full bank, expanded tutoring) is where it monetizes, so the "free" surface is smaller than the headline implies.

ExamCert (examcert.app). It advertises the biggest free number in this list — 500+ questions — and leans on freshness ("updated weekly") and a free PDF as the hook. If question volume is your priority, it is worth a look; just verify how much of the 500 is actually reachable without an upgrade before you rely on it.

CyberCertPrep. The differentiator here is hands-on: labs, flashcards, and simulations with no credit card required to start. That is exactly the gap pure multiple-choice banks leave open, because employers test tool skills, not just recall. The full course is paid, so use the free lab environment for the practical concepts and pair it with a free question bank.

PracticeTestGeeks. A broad test-prep site whose flashcards angle — no account, no sign-up, no paywall — makes it one of the most accessible free options. Coverage spans many certifications, so the CEH-specific depth is thinner than a dedicated v13 resource; best used as a fast free flashcard source rather than your entire plan.

Quizlet and similar DR-90 platforms. The advantage is authority plus a huge community deck library, which is why these rank so well. The weakness is accuracy: user-generated decks vary widely in quality and are not curated against the current v13 blueprint. Use them to spot extra terminology, but do not treat any single community deck as a complete or verified study source.

Infosec Institute / Simplilearn / StationX / Intellipaat. These own the "how to pass CEH exam" head term with genuinely good long-form content and strong domain authority (70–85). The catch is intent: their free articles, webinars, and samples are a top-of-funnel for paid training and courses. Read them for perspective, but do not expect a complete free path to the exam from any single one.

The Best Free Stack (How to Combine Them)

You do not have to pick one. A strong all-free plan stacks three tools so each covers the other's gap:

1. Structure — a schedule (CEHStudy) Start with the 30-day or 6-week plan, which sequences all 20 modules by exam weight so you spend the most time on Scanning, Footprinting, and Vulnerability Analysis first. A schedule is what turns a pile of free content into a plan with an exit date.
2. Recall — flashcards (CEHStudy + Quizlet) Use the module-organized deck for the core terms, and skim one or two community decks on Footprinting & Reconnaissance or Scanning Networks to catch terminology you have not seen. Revisit any module under 80% in the dashboard.
3. Depth — questions with explanations (CEHStudy + ExamCert/Open Exam Prep) Drill the 20 explained quizzes for learning, then widen your volume with a second bank. Read every explanation — understanding why is what moves you from 70% to 80%+.
4. Hands-on — free labs (CyberCertPrep / your own Kali box) Run a few practical sessions in Nmap, Metasploit, Wireshark, and Burp Suite against lab targets so the multiple-choice concepts become tools you can actually operate. This is the piece that also makes you hireable after the exam.

The exit criterion is the same regardless of which tools you stack: average 80%+ on timed practice across all 20 modules before you book. Until you can hold that line, add more volume and labs; once you can, schedule the exam (see our CEH v13 exam format for the 125-question, 4-hour, 60% spec).

Can I pass the CEH using only free resources?

Yes, if you study with a plan and put in the hours. The official EC-Council course is not required to pass. Is free CEH prep enough, or do I need a paid course?

Free prep is enough for the exam content. Paid courses mainly add structured video instruction, a proctor-like environment, and official lab time — useful if you learn best from video or your employer pays. But the concepts, question styles, and tools are all available for free: the trade-off is that you have to self-organize. If you follow a real schedule and test yourself honestly with practice questions, free resources cover the knowledge the CEH v13 exam tests.
