What Is the CEH Certification?

The EC-Council Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) v13 (exam code 312-50) is one of the most recognized cybersecurity certifications globally. It covers 20 domains including footprinting, scanning, enumeration, vulnerability analysis, system hacking, malware threats, sniffing, social engineering, session hijacking, web application attacks, cryptography, and more. The exam is 125 multiple-choice questions in 4 hours with a 60% passing score — no hands-on labs, which means conceptual knowledge of tools and techniques is what matters most.

What makes CEH unique among ethical-hacking credentials is its official recognition. It appears on the DoD Directive 8570.01 approved list for IAM Level II and IAT Level II positions, meaning it qualifies you for many government and defense-contractor roles in the United States. That single fact is why so many candidates ask whether it is worth it — the answer for those paths is almost certainly yes.

Salary Impact: Does CEH Really Increase Your Pay?

Certification ROI ultimately comes down to one question: does it help you earn more? The data says yes, with important context:

  • Glassdoor reports that cybersecurity professionals with CEH certification earn an average of $5,000–$15,000 more annually than peers without it.
  • Payscale data shows similar trends: CEH holders in the United States average roughly $95,000–$120,000 depending on experience and location.
  • The real multiplier is experience + certification. A security analyst with 2 years of hands-on work plus CEV typically negotiates a stronger title and salary than either alone.

The key insight: CEH is not a magic number on your resume. It is a force multiplier — it amplifies the value of experience you already have, making you more competitive for promotions and higher-level roles.

💡 The Smart Path

If you are completely new to security, start with Security+. It builds the networking and general security foundations CEV assumes you know. Once you have Security+ and want to specialize in offensive security, add CEH on top. That combination — Security+ for breadth, CEH for depth — is one of the most recognized pairings in the industry.

If you can only afford one right now, the choice depends on your goal — CEH for government/contractor roles and HR filters, OSCP for hands-on penetration-testing positions.

Who Should Get CEH (And Who Should Wait)

CEV is worth it if any of these describe you:

  • ✓ You want to work in offensive security (pentesting, red teaming)
  • ✓ You need a DoD-approved certification for government or contractor roles
  • ✓ You already have Security+ or equivalent experience and want to specialize
  • ✓ Your employer will reimburse the cost
  • ✓ You want a credential that clears HR filters at mid-to-large companies

Consider waiting if:

  • ❌ You have zero security background — start with Security+ or CompTIA Network+ first
  • ❌ Budget is tight right now — use our free study resources to build knowledge while you save
  • ❌ You want pure hands-on validation — OSCP or eJPT may be a better fit

Frequently Asked Questions

Is CEH or Security+ better for getting a job?

Security+ is the better first certification because it builds networking and general security foundations at a lower cost ($370 exam). CEH is the better second certification when you already have Security+ or equivalent experience — HR filters for CEH in offensive-security roles, and many federal contracts require it by name. If you can only afford one right now, start with Security+. If you already have fundamentals and want an offensive-security credential, go straight to CEH.

How much does the CEH certification increase your salary?

Salary data varies by source and experience level, but Glassdoor and Payscale consistently report that professionals with a CEH earn $5,000–$15,000 more annually than peers without it. The real ROI multiplier is combining CEH with existing experience: a security analyst with 2 years of hands-on work plus CEH typically negotiates a stronger title and salary than either alone.

Does the DoD accept the CEH certification?

Yes. The CEH v13 (312-50) is on the DoD Directive 8570.01 approved list for IAM Level II and IAT Level II positions, meaning it qualifies you for many government and defense-contractor roles in the United States. This is one of the certification's strongest advantages — few other ethical-hacking credentials carry the same official recognition.

Is CEH harder than OSCP?

OSCP is significantly harder. It is a fully hands-on 24-hour practical exam where you must actually exploit machines, while CEH is purely multiple-choice testing conceptual knowledge of tools and techniques. Most professionals treat them as complementary: CEH for HR screening and OSCP to prove real-world offensive skills.

How long does the CEH certification last?

The CEH certification is valid for 3 years, after which you must earn Continuing Professional Education (CPE) credits to renew it. EC-Council also offers an Alumni status that lets you retain your certified name and number while the credential shows as inactive — useful if you are between roles.

Can I pass CEH with free resources only?

Yes. The official EC-Council course is not required to pass the exam. A structured self-study path using free practice questions, flashcards, and module notes is sufficient for most candidates. The key is consistent daily contact with material — short sessions of practice questions plus flashcard review outperform weekend cramming.
