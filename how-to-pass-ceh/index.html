How to Pass the CEH v13 Exam in 2026: Step-by-Step Strategy

Last updated August 2026 · 6 steps from first-day assessment to exam day

Reviewed by Chris Pardue, CEH · Certified security practitioner · Last reviewed August 12, 2026

Passing the CEH (312-50) is less about genius and more about process. The exam is a 125-question, 4-hour multiple-choice test with a 60% passing line — clear enough to beat if you follow a structure most candidates never bother to define. This guide lays out the exact sequence: understand the format, baseline yourself honestly, run a week-by-week plan, drill your weak modules until they stop costing you points, build minimal hands-on skills on the tools the exam loves, and execute disciplined exam-day tactics. Every action step below links to a free CEHStudy tool so you can start today — no account, no paywall.

Step 1: Know the CEH v13 Exam Format

Before scheduling anything, internalize what you are walking into. The CEH v13 exam (code 312-50) is delivered by Pearson VUE in a test center or online proctored:

SpecCEH v13 (312-50)
Questions125 multiple choice (single and multiple answer)
Time limit4 hours (~1 min 53 sec per question)
Passing score60% — 75 correct answers
ContentAll 20 CEH v13 modules/domains
Voucher price$1,250 (member) / $1,450 (non-member), valid 6 months
RetakeAllowed after 30 days with a new voucher

Three implications matter for strategy. First, you only need 75 of 125 correct — you can afford to lose a third of the exam, which is why coverage beats depth in the smaller domains. Second, at ~1 minute 53 seconds per question, speed is the hidden grading criterion; slow readers run out of clock before running out of knowledge. Third, all 20 modules appear on the exam, so a study plan that skips the 5% domains literally leaves 3–6 passable questions on the table. Our CEH v13 exam domains breakdown has the full weighting per module — start there to see which areas earn the most questions.

Step 2: Self-Assessment (Do This Before You Book)

Most candidates over-estimate where they stand. The cheapest way to fix that is data, not confidence. Take a diagnostic pass before you build any schedule:

1. Run the full 20-module diagnostic Our free 200-question practice bank has 10 questions per module. Work through all 20 — that is roughly an hour of work — and record your raw score per module. Do not study explanations deeply yet; you are measuring, not learning.
2. Sort the modules into three buckets Strong (80%+): maintenance review only. Medium (50–79%): focused study plus flashcards. Weak (below 50%): full re-study of the module notes, then redrill. Candidates who never write this list down typically keep grinding their strongest modules and ignore the ones costing them points.
3. Set a booking threshold Do not buy the voucher until a second full diagnostic shows 80%+ average across all modules, with no module below 60%. Booking before that threshold is how people spend $1,250–$1,450 to fund a retake. Remember: once you buy the voucher it expires in 6 months, so the calendar pressure pushes you toward testing unready.

If the diagnostic is uniformly bad — everything under 50% — that signals a fundamentals gap (networking, TCP/IP, basic security concepts) rather than an exam problem. Fix the foundation first with our CEH v13 study guide and the early modules (Introduction to Ethical Hacking, Footprinting & Reconnaissance) before running the plan below.

This 6-week schedule assumes ~1–2 hours per day. It sequences modules by exam weight, front-loads the high-yield areas (footprinting, scanning, and vulnerability analysis carry the most questions), and keeps daily drill time separate from learning time. Two rules make this work. Rule one: daily contact. Even on heavy weeks, never skip the 15-minute drill — recall decays fast and the flashcard app's progress tracker exists to expose those gaps. Rule two: mock passes in week 6 must be timed. Untimed practice flatters your score; a timed pass of 100+ out of 125-equivalent difficulty at or under 4 hours is the single best predictor of passing. If your timed mock sits above 85%, book the exam. If it wobbles between 80–85%, add three days and re-test.

Step 4: Attack Weak Areas With the Free Question Bank

Practice questions are where strategy becomes points. The mistake most people make is random drilling — opening any quiz at random. The effective loop is targeted and repeatable:

  1. Go to your weakest bucket first. Pull the module's page from the practice hub — each of the 20 modules has a dedicated quiz with 10 questions and a full explanation for every answer.
  2. Read every explanation, even on correct answers. The explanation tells you why the right answer is right and — more valuably — why each distractor sounds plausible. CEH distractors are written to catch exactly the half-understood concept, so the distractor analysis is the actual lesson.
  3. Convert misses into flashcards in your head. For every question you got wrong, identify the single fact that decided it (a port number, a scan type, an algorithm property) and review that fact the same day. Our 306-card flashcard deck covers most of these atomic facts — load your weak modules there.
  4. Redrill 48 hours later. Spaced repetition is why the bank reshuffles and why week 6 exists. A module at 70% today should be retested, not assumed fixed.

Track it simply: one spreadsheet row per module (score, date, notes). When every row reads 80%+, stop expanding and start consolidating — consolidation in the final week is what turns 80% practice scores into 75+ on the real test.

Step 5: Build Hands-On Lab Skills (Minimal, High-Yield)

The CEH exam tests tool behavior conceptually — what a command does, what its output means, which tool fits which phase of an engagement. You do not need to become an operator; you need hands-on fluency on the five tools that appear in the most questions:

  • Kali Linux (or Parrot OS): run it in a VM (VirtualBox is free) so you know the layout of the tooling by category — information gathering, password attacks, web applications, wireless. The exam references tool locations and defaults, not just names.
  • Nmap: the single highest-value lab hour in the whole certification. Until you can explain from memory: SYN scan (-sS), version detection (-sV), OS detection (-O), service scripts (-sC/--script), and what a stealthy scan looks like versus a connect scan, keep practicing. Read our Scanning Networks module alongside.
  • Metasploit: learn the loop: search for an exploit → check the vuln → set payloads and hosts → run → get a session. Know the difference between an exploit, a payload, and a meterpreter session — exam questions phrase all three.
  • Wireshark: do packet analysis until the TCP handshake (SYN → SYN-ACK → ACK) and common protocols are second nature. Practice identifying FTP cleartext credentials, spotting a man-in-the-middle signature, and reading a capture filtered to one conversation.
  • Burp Suite: intercept, modify, and replay one request end-to-end. Then you can answer questions about reflected vs stored XSS detection, basic SQLi probing, and how the proxy actually works — see the Hacking Web Applications module.

If you have no lab environment at all, free platforms like TryHackMe and Hack The Box cover these exact toolchains legally and cheaply — two to three hours per week on them during weeks 1–5 is enough. The exam will never ask you to exploit a live system; it asks whether you know what happens when you do. That knowledge transfers one-to-one from any legal lab.

Step 6: Exam-Day Tactics

You have 4 hours for 125 questions. That budget breaks down to about 1 minute 53 seconds per question, and the candidates who fail at 74/125 usually lost more time than they lost knowledge. Execute these tactics:

Tactic 1: Two-pass technique First pass: answer every question you can do in under 90 seconds, flag the rest. Second pass: work only flagged items with your remaining time. This prevents one hard question from eating five minutes that belonged to three easy ones.
Tactic 2: Read the last line first Long CEH questions bury the actual ask in the final sentence. Scene-setting is context; the question is the ask. Reading bottom-up cuts your effective question time in half.
Tactic 3: Eliminate before you choose On single-answer items, knock out the two obviously wrong options first — the real decision is almost always between the remaining two. For multiple-answer questions, answer each option independently (true/false) rather than looking for a "theme."
Tactic 4: Reserve the last 10 minutes No new answers in the final 10 minutes. Use them to revisit flags, confirm no questions are blank, and sanity-check your two hardest guesses. A blank question is a guaranteed miss; a considered guess is not.
Tactic 5: Logistics before the login Online proctored: run the system check the day before, confirm ID matches your profile exactly, and clear your desk and camera view. Test center: arrive 30 minutes early. Half of "exam day disasters" are administrative — do not make one of them yours.

📌 The One-Number Rule

Track only one number all the way to exam day: your average score across full mock passes. It is above 85% for two consecutive timed mocks — book the voucher. It is not — stay in the plan. Every other metric (hours studied, modules "finished", flashcards reviewed) feels productive but none of them predict the pass.

Frequently Asked Questions

How hard is the CEH v13 exam?

The CEH v13 (312-50) exam is 125 multiple-choice questions in 4 hours with a 60% passing score (75 correct). Candidates with prior security experience or CompTIA Security+ typically find it manageable in 3–4 weeks. Beginners should budget 6 weeks of consistent study. Most failures come from poor time management and neglecting the smaller-weighted modules rather than from genuinely impossible questions.

How long should I study for the CEH exam?

Plan 6 weeks at 1–2 hours per day, or 3–4 weeks at 2–3 hours per day if you have a security background. The deciding factor is not total hours but consistent daily contact with the material: short daily sessions of practice questions plus flashcard review outperform weekend cramming. Book your voucher once a full mock pass sits at 80%+ average, not before.

Can I pass CEH with self-study and free resources?

Yes. A structured self-study path works if you (1) follow a week-by-week plan covering all 20 modules, (2) drill weak areas with explanation-backed practice questions — our free bank has 200 across the 20 modules, (3) review concepts on flashcards, and (4) build at least one hands-on lab skill per high-yield tool (Nmap, Metasploit, Wireshark, Burp Suite). The official EC-Council course is not required to pass.

What are the best CEH exam-day tactics?

Budget roughly 1 minute 53 seconds per question. Do two passes: answer what you know confidently, flag uncertain items, and return to them with remaining time. Eliminate obviously wrong options before choosing — on single-answer questions that usually narrows it to two real candidates. Read the last line of long questions first, since they contain the actual ask. Do not sit in the final 10 minutes guessing; use that time for unanswered flags.

What score do I need to pass the CEH exam?

The passing score is 60% — 75 out of 125 questions. Results are available immediately at the end of the exam. If you miss, you can retake after 30 days with a new voucher. Target 80–85% consistently on practice sets before test day so that normal exam-day nerves still leave you safely above the line.

How much does the CEH v13 exam cost?

The exam voucher costs $1,250 USD for EC-Council members and $1,450 for non-members. The voucher is valid for 6 months and covers one attempt at the 125-question exam via Pearson VUE, in a test center or online proctored. Annual membership renewal runs about $195, so joining as a member pays off on voucher pricing alone if you will take the exam within a year.
