CEH vs CompTIA Security+ vs CISSP — Which Cybersecurity Certification is Right for You?

Choosing the right cybersecurity certification can significantly impact your career trajectory. The CEH (Certified Ethical Hacker), CompTIA Security+, and CISSP (Certified Information Systems Security Professional) are three of the most sought-after credentials in the field — but they serve different career paths, experience levels, and salary ranges.

CEH vs Security+ — Detailed Comparison

What Each Certification Covers

CompTIA Security+ covers six broad domains: cybersecurity architecture, threat management, identity and access management, cryptography, operations and incident response, and software development security. It's designed as a foundational certification for anyone entering the cybersecurity field.

CEH v13 covers 20 specific offensive security domains including footprinting, scanning, vulnerability analysis, system hacking, malware, social engineering, web application attacks, wireless hacking, mobile security, IoT/OT, cloud security, and cryptography. It's designed for professionals who will actively perform penetration testing.

Career Path Differences

Choose Security+ if: You're new to cybersecurity (0-1 years experience), need an entry-level certification to get your first security job, or want broad foundational knowledge across multiple security domains.
Choose CEH if: You have 1+ years of IT/security experience and want to specialize in offensive security (penetration testing, vulnerability assessment, red teaming). CEH is the industry standard for pentester positions.

Which Should You Get First?

Complete beginner (no IT background): Start with CompTIA A+ → Security+ → then CEH. This builds the foundation you need.

IT professional moving into security: Go straight for CEH if targeting pentesting roles, or Security+ first if targeting analyst/GRC roles.

Security professional specializing in offense: Get CEH immediately. It's the most directly applicable certification to pentesting work.

CEH vs CISSP — Detailed Comparison

The CISSP is a management-level certification covering eight domains of security architecture and program management. Unlike CEH, it requires 5 years of paid experience in two or more CISSP domains. This makes it unsuitable for early-career professionals but ideal for those ready to move into leadership positions.

Choose CISSP if: You have 5+ years of security experience and want to move into CISO, security architect, or senior security manager roles. It's the gold standard for security leadership positions.

Certification Stacking Strategy

Offensive Security Path (Pentester/Red Team)

  1. CompTIA A+ (if no IT background) — 4 weeks
  2. CompTIA Security+ — 4-6 weeks
  3. CEH v13 — 8-12 weeks
  4. OSCP (optional, advanced) — 3-4 months

Security Management Path (CISO Track)

  1. CompTIA Security+ — 4-6 weeks
  2. Gain 5 years security experience
  3. CISSP — 4-6 months
  4. CISM or CISA (optional, specialized) — 2-3 months

Cloud Security Path

  1. CompTIA Security+ or AWS Certified Solutions Architect
  2. CEH v13 (for offensive cloud security) or AWS Security Specialty
  3. CISSP (after 5 years, for leadership)

Is CEH Worth It in 2026?

Yes. The CEH maintains strong industry recognition for several reasons:

  • Job requirements: Many penetration testing and red team positions list CEH as a preferred or required qualification
  • Government contracts: FedRAMP and DoD-related security positions often require CEH or equivalent
  • Global recognition: Unlike some certifications, CEH is widely recognized internationally
  • Salary impact: CEH holders report 10-20% higher starting salaries than non-certified peers in pentesting roles

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I get a job with only CEH and no other certifications?
A: Yes, but it's challenging without any prior IT/security experience. Most employers expect at minimum 1-2 years of hands-on experience or a related degree alongside the CEH. Pairing CEH with Security+ significantly improves your job prospects.

Q: Do I need to renew the CEH certification?
A: The CEH certification itself doesn't expire, but to maintain active EC-Council membership (which is expected by employers), you must earn 40 CPE credits annually and pay a ~$195 annual renewal fee.

Q: Is CompTIA PenTest+ a good alternative to CEH?
A: PenTest+ ($392) is a solid intermediate alternative for those who find CEH too expensive or want a CompTIA-aligned path. However, CEH has broader industry recognition, particularly in government and enterprise environments. If budget is a concern, PenTest+ + Security+ can be a strong combination.

Q: How long should I study for each exam?
A: Security+: 4-8 weeks at 1-2 hours daily. CEH: 6-12 weeks at 1-2 hours daily (use our free 6-week study plan). CISSP: 3-6 months at 1-2 hours daily. Adjust based on your existing experience level.
