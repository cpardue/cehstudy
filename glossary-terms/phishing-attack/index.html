Phishing Attack

A Phishing Attack is a social engineering technique where an attacker disguises themselves as a trusted entity (via email, SMS, phone, or other communication channels) to trick victims into revealing sensitive information such as credentials, financial data, or personally identifiable information (PII). It is the most common initial vector for cyberattacks.

Types & Variations

  • Spear Phishing — Targeted attack on a specific individual or organization using personalized information gathered through reconnaissance
  • Whaling — Spear phishing specifically targeting C-suite executives and high-profile individuals
  • Smishing — Phishing via SMS/text messages, often containing malicious links or fake verification requests
  • Vishing — Voice phishing via phone calls impersonating banks, tech support, or government agencies
  • Deceptive Phishing — Generic mass emails using cloned logos, URLs, and content from legitimate brands
  • Baiting — Phishing luring victims with the promise of free something (USB drives, downloads) to install malware

Common Tools & Techniques

GoPhish (open-source phishing framework) King Phisher Social-Engineer's Toolkit (SET) Email headers analysis

Ethical hackers and security professionals use these tools in authorized penetration tests and red team operations. Unauthorized use is illegal under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) and equivalent legislation worldwide.

Real-World Examples

Credential Harvesting: Fake "password expired" email linking to a lookalike login page that captures entered credentials
Malware Delivery: "Invoice attached" spear phish with macro-enabled document that downloads a reverse shell payload
Business Email Compromise (BEC): Forged executive email instructing finance to wire funds to attacker-controlled account

How Phishing Attack Appears on the CEH v13 Exam

Phishing is covered extensively in CEH v13 Module 09 (Social Engineering). The exam tests knowledge of all phishing variants, the difference between phishing and pretexting, email header analysis for spoofing detection, user education strategies, technical countermeasures (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), and how to design and evaluate a corporate phishing simulation program. Expect multiple questions covering both offensive and defensive perspectives.

Prevention & Detection

  • Input Validation: Implement strict allowlist-based input validation on all user-facing parameters
  • Principle of Least Privilege: Run applications with minimum required permissions
  • Regular Patching: Maintain timely vulnerability management and patch deployment
  • Network Segmentation: Limit lateral movement through zone-based network architecture
  • Monitoring & Alerting: Deploy SIEM/SOAR solutions with threat detection rules for anomalous activity
