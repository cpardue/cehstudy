CEH Study Plan: 30-Day & 6-Week Schedules with Labs

Last updated August 2026 · Two full schedules, one module order

Reviewed by Chris Pardue, CEH · Certified security practitioner · Last reviewed August 12, 2026

"How long should I study for the CEH?" is the wrong question — "which schedule fits my calendar?" is the right one. This page gives you both: a 30-day intensive CEH v13 study plan for candidates with an exam date inside a month, and a 6-week CEH study schedule for those studying alongside a full-time job. Both cover the same 20 modules in the same order — sequenced by exam weight so week one hits where ~40% of questions come from — with hands-on labs built into every phase. Want the lighter theory-only version? See the simpler 6-week CEH study plan; this page is its lab-integrated, expanded form plus the 30-day track.

How to Choose Your Schedule

Pick the schedule that matches your constraint, not your ambition. The honest test is daily hours: the 30-day plan is a ~6-hour commitment for a month, and it only works if that fits your normal week.

Your situationRecommended scheduleWhy
Exam date fixed within ~30 days; can study full-time (≈6 h/day)30-day planCovers all 20 modules plus mock exams in the time you have.
Exam in 4–8 weeks; working full-time, can do ≈2.5 h/day6-week planSame coverage at sustainable intensity with built-in buffer.
Complete beginner to security, any start date6-week plan (stretch to 8 if needed)Front-loaded high-weight modules give early wins; pace leaves room to re-study.
Taking it again after a failed attempt30-day plan, or 6-week if your first attempt showed broad gapsRetakes usually need targeted drilling — both schedules end in mock-exam mode.
The one rule both schedules share Do not buy the voucher until you average 80%+ on timed practice (125 questions, 4 hours, no module below ~60%). If a mock lands below that line, shift the date and re-run the weak modules — the fee makes an unready booking far more expensive than two extra weeks. Full logic: How to Pass CEH v13.

Assumes ~6 hours per day: 2.5 h reading + flashcards, ≥1 h lab, 2 h practice questions with explanations. Phases follow exam weight — week one is where the most points live — and the final five days are mock-exam and recovery, not new material. Hands-On Labs: The Non-Negotiable 30 Minutes

The CEH exam is multiple-choice only — no labs, no typed commands. Both schedules still require lab time every phase because the questions test recognizing what a tool does and what its output means, and that comes from having done it. Five labs cover almost everything asked:

  • Nmap (Days 2–5 / Week 1): SYN, UDP, FIN, XMAS, NULL scans plus -sV version detection against your own test host. This single lab is worth ~30 questions between Scanning and Footprinting.
  • Vulnerability scanning (Days 7–8 / Week 2): OpenVAS or Nessus Essentials on a vulnerable VM (Metasploitable in a sandbox), then CVSS-score 5 findings by hand before comparing to the scanner's scores.
  • Password & privilege-escalation drill (Days 9–10 / Week 2): Hydra and John the Ripper against a lab account, then one local privilege-escalation path end to end — know which method fits which scenario.
  • Wireshark capture (Day 12 / Week 3): 50+ packets, with a labeled TCP handshake, an ARP exchange, and one suspicious flow. Packet questions reward people who have read real captures, not textbook diagrams.
  • Burp Suite + DVWA (Days 14–16 / Week 4): one XSS, one SQL injection, and one path traversal in an authorized vulnerable app. Knowing what each attack type looks like in the console is exactly what the web module tests.

Everything runs on your own machines, lab VMs, or sanctioned training platforms — never against systems you do not own or have written permission to test. No hardware budget? Free browser-based practice environments and home wi-fi (for the wireless module) are enough for all of the above.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 30 days enough to pass the CEH exam?

Yes — if you can commit about 6 hours per day and have at least some security background. The exam is 125 multiple-choice questions in 4 hours with a 60% passing score, so the barrier is breadth and pace, not depth. Complete beginners should take the 6-week plan (or stretch the 30-day plan to 40) rather than risk an under-prepared voucher. In both schedules, book only after hitting an 80%+ average on timed practice.

How many hours a day do I need for the 30-day CEH plan?

Roughly 6 hours per day: about 2.5 hours of reading and flashcards, at least 1 hour of hands-on lab work, and 2 hours of practice questions with explanations. If you cannot sustain 5+ hours daily, use the 6-week plan instead — it covers the same 20 modules at about 2.5 hours per day.

Which CEH study schedule should I choose: 30 days or 6 weeks?

Choose 30 days if your exam date is fixed inside a month, you can study full-time (~6 hours/day), and you have some networking or security base. Choose 6 weeks if you work full-time, are a complete beginner, or prefer steady progress over intensity. Both use the same weight-sequenced module order and lab set, so either fully covers the v13 blueprint.

Do I need hands-on labs to pass the CEH exam?

You are not tested in a lab — the CEH is pure multiple choice. But labs make tool and technique questions familiar fast: running Nmap scan types yourself, reading real Wireshark captures, and firing up Burp Suite against an authorized vulnerable app all turn abstract questions into pattern recognition. Both schedules build at least one lab into every phase.

What should I study first for the CEH v13 exam?

The highest-weighted domains: Footprinting & Reconnaissance (~15%), Scanning Networks (~15%), and Vulnerability Analysis (~10%) — together about 40% of the exam. The first week of both schedules front-loads exactly these plus Introduction to Ethical Hacking, so your time is spent where the most questions come from.
