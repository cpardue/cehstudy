CEH v13 Study Guide: 20 Domains, 6-Week & 30-Day Schedules (2026)

Last updated August 2026 · CEH v13 (exam 312-50)

Reviewed by the CEHStudy team · Certified security practitioners · Last reviewed August 30, 2026

This is the central guide for preparing for the EC-Council Certified Ethical Hacker v13 (312-50) exam. It does three jobs: it lists all 20 exam domains in curriculum order with a link to every free resource we hold for each one, and it gives you two complete day-by-day schedules you can follow from module one to exam day — a 6-week plan for people studying 1-2 hours most weekdays, and a 30-day intensive for people who can put in four or more hours daily. Both schedules run the modules in the same order, because they build on each other: reconnaissance and scanning come before exploitation, exploitation before post-exploitation work, and cryptography closes the list as the theoretical spine that every other domain leans on.

Why This Order: How the Exam Is Built Around It

The domain list above is not alphabetical and it is not ranked by popularity — it follows the attack lifecycle the 312-50 exam is constructed around. Information gathering (footprinting, scanning, enumeration) comes first because later questions assume you can already discover and map a target: a traffic-analysis question takes for granted that you know how to capture and read packets, and lateral-movement questions assume initial access already happened. Studying in dependency order means every new domain has ground under it instead of a gap.

The exam also does not weigh all 20 domains equally, and EC-Council does not publish a fixed per-domain percentage chart for v13 that we could verify, so these schedules encode the weighting into time allocation instead. Count the hours each domain receives in the tables below: the four densest attack areas — malware, traffic analysis, IDS evasion, and web servers — get double allocations in the intensive and paired lab work in the weekly plan, while lighter domains take a single day. That distribution is the scheduling answer to "what does the exam care about most." For per-domain topic breakdowns, see the CEH v13 exam domains page.

Two practical consequences. First, when your real week runs ahead or behind the table, spend the surplus hours on the domain where your practice-question accuracy is lowest — that is almost always worth more than finishing a strong domain early. Second, cryptography deliberately closes the list: it is the theoretical spine the other domains lean on, and placing it late keeps its concepts fresh right before exam day while its lab work (hashing and encryption) keeps it concrete rather than abstract.

6-Week vs 30-Day: Which One Is Yours?

Both tables above cover the identical 20 modules in the identical order, so the decision comes down to arithmetic and a consistency record rather than content. The math: the intensive's table allocates exactly 120 hours across its 30 days, while the 6-week plan lands somewhere between 42 and 84 focused hours depending on whether you average one or two per day. If your realistic weekly availability sits near that gap, pick the plan that matches your calendar — then defend it.

  • Choose the 6-week plan if: you work full-time and can protect one or two steady evening hours on most weekdays; your exam date is six weeks out or later (you book it after Week 6); you are newer to security and need 42 days of spaced repetition rather than a month of intensity.
  • Choose the 30-day intensive if: you can guarantee four or more hours daily for a full month — a gap between jobs, reduced hours, or a dedicated study block; you already have hands-on security experience and mainly need structured revision; your exam date falls within roughly five weeks; your track record says you can hold daily consistency without skipped days.

A hybrid works well: start on the 6-week plan and, if you are consistently finishing each day's block early, pull later modules forward to compress toward the intensive. Run it backwards when you fall behind — slide Week 6 and move your exam date rather than cramming. The one rule that matters more than all the others: never run the intensive at two hours a day. That is not a slow version of the 30-day plan, it is a broken 45-day plan with no slack to absorb missed days. If your honest availability is around two hours daily, take the 6-week schedule and book a later date; rescheduling mechanics are covered on the CEH exam registration page.

Common CEH Study Scheduling Mistakes

Most failed study plans are not defeated by difficulty but by scheduling habits that compound quietly. These are the ones we see most often, with the fix each one already has in the tables above.

  • Starting without a baseline. Diving into Module 1 treats your strengths and gaps identically for six weeks. Before Day 1, run one practice set per module — ten questions each, free on every module page — note the modules that score lowest, and lean your review time toward them.
  • Front-loading the easy wins. Introduction and reconnaissance modules feel productive fast, so people burn through Week 1 early and arrive at the dense attack domains with half their scheduled hours. Follow the hour allocations in the tables, not enthusiasm — the heavier domains already carry extra days and labs for a reason.
  • Letting missed days compound. One skipped day is invisible; three create a gap that weekly reviews cannot absorb, and the intensive has zero slack. When you miss a day, do the next day's block as make-up or slide everything down one day — decide in the moment, not "I will catch up later."
  • Trading practice questions for flashcards. Flipping cards feels like studying, but recognition is not recall under time pressure. Both schedules pair every module with its ten practice questions on purpose; treat them as required work, not optional review.
  • Skipping the timed mock until it is too late. High volume without time pressure hides pacing problems right up to exam day. The 6-week plan puts two full-length mocks in Week 6 and the intensive reserves Day 29 for exactly this — protect those slots, then spend the following day only on what you missed.
  • Leaving exam logistics to exam week. Voucher confirmation, test-center location, and ID requirements discovered the day before are a classic self-inflicted failure. Both schedules park logistics in their own dedicated row (Day 41 in the weekly plan, Day 30 in the intensive) — do it there and stop thinking about it.
  • Pride-sitting in the wrong plan. Staying on the intensive at two hours a day, or dragging the 6-week plan to eight weeks "because you are close," both end worse than switching. The plans differ only in compression; when your real hours stop matching the plan you chose, switch and move the date instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I study for the CEH v13?

Most candidates following the 6-week schedule study 1-2 hours a day across 42 days. The 30-day intensive assumes 4 or more hours every day. Choose based on your calendar, not just the exam date.

Can I realistically pass the CEH in 30 days?

Yes, if you can dedicate 4+ hours daily for a month. The 30-day plan compresses all 20 modules into daily blocks, adds checkpoint reviews, and finishes with a full-length mock on Day 29. With fewer available hours, the 6-week schedule is the safer choice.

Which schedule should I pick — 6 weeks or 30 days?

Use the 6-week plan if you can commit 1-2 focused hours most weekdays. Use the 30-day intensive if you have four or more hours daily and your exam date is within a month. Both cover the same 20 modules in the same order.
