What Is the CEH Certification?

The Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) certification is offered by EC-Council and is one of the most recognized ethical hacking certifications globally. It validates your ability to think like a hacker — to identify vulnerabilities in IT systems before malicious actors can exploit them.

CEH v13 is the latest version of the exam, updated to reflect modern threat landscapes including cloud security, IoT/OT hacking, and advanced lateral movement techniques. The exam code is 312-50.

Who Should Get CEH Certified?

  • Security Analysts / Engineers — Professionals who need hands-on penetration testing skills
  • IT Administrators — System admins who want to understand offensive security
  • SOC Analysts — Security operations team members who investigate attacks
  • Penetration Testers — Those entering the ethical hacking profession
  • Security Consultants — Advisors who assess client security postures
  • Students — Those pursuing cybersecurity careers

CEH Exam Details

  • Exam Code: 312-50
  • Format: Multiple choice, 125 questions
  • Duration: 4 hours
  • Passing Score: 60% (75 out of 125)
  • Cost: $1,199 USD (exam voucher); $849 for EC-Council students
  • Prerequisites: 2 years of IT security experience OR EC-Council training certification
  • Delivery: Pearson VUE test centers or online proctoring

How to Study for the CEH Exam

Step 1: Understand the Exam Blueprint

Before diving into study materials, review the official EC-Council exam objectives. Know exactly which topics will be tested and their weight percentages. This helps you allocate study time proportionally — spend more hours on Scanning (20%) than on Evading IDS (5%).

Step 2: Build Foundational Knowledge

Start with Module 1: Introduction to Ethical Hacking to understand the legal and ethical framework. Then move through modules in order, building your knowledge progressively.

Step 3: Use Our Free Flashcards

Our flashcards are organized by module and designed for efficient memorization. Key features:

  • Selective studying: Focus on one or multiple modules at a time
  • Randomized order: No repeats per session to test true knowledge
  • Mobile-friendly: Study on any device, anywhere
  • Completely free: No signup required

Step 4: Practice with Hands-On Labs

The CEH exam tests practical knowledge. Supplement your reading with:

  • Virtual lab environments (TryHackMe, HackTheBox)
  • Practice penetration testing in isolated networks
  • Command-line tool practice (Nmap, Metasploit, Wireshark)
  • Web application vulnerability labs (OWASP WebGoat, DVWA)

Step 5: Review and Reinforce

In the final week before your exam:

  • Review all flashcard modules at least twice
  • Focus on weak areas identified during practice tests
  • Re-read key definitions and cryptographic concepts
  • Practice timeline estimation for scanning phases

Don't rush through Modules 02 and 03 — they form the foundation for the entire ethical hacking methodology. Spend extra time on footprinting, scanning tools, and port numbers. These topics alone account for 35% of the exam.

CEH Study Tips from Experienced Test Takers

  1. Memorize port numbers — Common ports (21, 22, 23, 25, 53, 80, 443, 445, 3389) and their associated services appear frequently
  2. Understand scan types — Know the difference between TCP connect, SYN stealth, FIN, XMAS, and NULL scans cold
  3. Learn tool flags — Nmap switches (-sS, -sV, -O, -A), Metasploit modules, Wireshark filters
  4. Cryptography basics — Symmetric vs asymmetric algorithms, hash functions, digital signatures, PKI
  5. Think like an attacker — CEH tests offensive mindset; practice thinking about how vulnerabilities chain together
  6. Eliminate wrong answers — Multiple choice questions often have 2 obviously wrong options; focus on the remaining 2
  7. Watch for keywords — "BEST", "FIRST", "MOST appropriate" change the correct answer significantly

Common CEH Topics That Appear Every Exam

  • Footprinting tools: Whois, NSlookup, Dig, Wireshark, Maltego, TheHarvester
  • Scanning commands: Nmap syntax and output interpretation
  • TCP handshake: SYN, SYN-ACK, ACK — and how each scan type interacts with it
  • Password attacks: Brute force, dictionary, rainbow tables, salting
  • SQL injection types: Union-based, blind, boolean-based, time-based
  • XSS categories: Reflected, stored, DOM-based
  • Wireless attacks: KRACK, Evil Twin, Bluejacking/Bluesnarfing
  • Social engineering vectors: Phishing, vishing, smishing, tailgating, pretexting

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to prepare for the CEH exam?

Most candidates spend 4–8 weeks preparing, studying 10–15 hours per week. If you already have security experience (Security+, CISSP), you may need less time. Complete beginners should plan for the full 6–8 weeks.

Is CEH v13 harder than v12?

v13 introduces new topics like IoT/OT hacking, cloud security, and lateral movement while removing some legacy content. The difficulty is comparable but the exam now reflects more modern attack vectors. Focus on the new domains if you're switching from v12.

Can I pass CEH with just flashcards?

Flashcards are excellent for memorizing key terms, port numbers, tool names, and scan types — but they're not sufficient alone. Combine them with hands-on labs, official study guides, and practice exams for the best chance of passing.

How much does the CEH exam cost?

The standalone exam voucher costs $1,199 USD. If you enroll in EC-Council's official training (CET), the exam is included for $849. Third-party vouchers may be available through authorized resellers at discounted rates.

Can I use these free flashcards on my phone?

Yes! Our flashcard app is fully responsive and works on any device — desktop, tablet, or smartphone. No app download required; just open the URL in your mobile browser.
