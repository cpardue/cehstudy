What Is the CEH Certification?

The Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) certification is offered by EC-Council and is one of the most recognized ethical hacking certifications globally. It validates your ability to think like a hacker — to identify vulnerabilities in IT systems before malicious actors can exploit them. CEH is the gold standard for entry-level penetration testing careers.

CEH v13 (exam code 312-50) is the latest version of the exam, updated to reflect modern threat landscapes including cloud security, IoT/OT hacking, lateral movement techniques, and advanced exploitation. This free CEH study guide covers every domain tested on the exam with detailed explanations.

Who Should Get CEH Certified?

  • Security Analysts / Engineers — Professionals who need hands-on penetration testing and offensive security skills
  • IT Administrators — System admins who want to understand offensive security and protect their infrastructure
  • SOC Analysts — Security operations team members who investigate attacks and need to think like attackers
  • Penetration Testers — Those entering the ethical hacking profession or validating their skills
  • Security Consultants — Advisors who assess client security postures and need CEH credibility
  • Students — Those pursuing cybersecurity careers and building foundational ethical hacking knowledge

CEH Exam Details

  • Exam Code: 312-50
  • Format: Multiple choice, 125 questions
  • Duration: 4 hours
  • Passing Score: 60% (75 out of 125)
  • Cost: $1,199 USD (exam voucher); $849 for EC-Council students
  • Prerequisites: 2 years of IT security experience OR EC-Council training certification
  • Delivery: Pearson VUE test centers or online proctoring

CEH Study Tips from Experienced Test Takers

  1. Memorize port numbers cold — Common ports (21 FTP, 22 SSH, 23 Telnet, 25 SMTP, 53 DNS, 80 HTTP, 443 HTTPS, 445 SMB, 3389 RDP) and their associated services appear on every CEH exam
  2. Understand scan types inside out — Know the difference between TCP connect ( -sT), SYN stealth (-sS), FIN (-sF), XMAS (-sX), and NULL (-sN) scans cold. Nmap is the most tested tool on the exam
  3. Learn tool flags and commands — Nmap switches (-sS, -sV, -O, -A, -p), Metasploit modules (auxiliary, exploit, payload), Wireshark display filters
  4. Cryptography basics are essential — Symmetric vs asymmetric algorithms (AES, RSA), hash functions (MD5, SHA-256), digital signatures, PKI infrastructure
  5. Think like an attacker — CEH tests offensive mindset; practice thinking about how vulnerabilities chain together from footprinting through post-exploitation
  6. Eliminate wrong answers strategically — Multiple choice questions often have 2 obviously wrong options; focus on the remaining 2 to increase your odds
  7. Watch for keywords in questions — "BEST", "FIRST", "MOST appropriate" change the correct answer significantly. Read every option carefully

Common CEH Exam Topics That Appear Every Single Exam

  • Footprinting tools: Whois, NSlookup, Dig, Wireshark, Maltego, TheHarvester, Shodan — reconnaissance techniques and OSINT methods
  • Scanning commands: Nmap syntax, output interpretation, scan types (-sS SYN, -sV version, -O OS detection)
  • TCP handshake sequence: SYN → SYN-ACK → ACK — and how each scan type interacts with the three-way handshake
  • Password attacks: Brute force, dictionary, rainbow tables, salting, PBKDF2, LM hashes
  • SQL injection types: Union-based, blind (boolean-based), time-based blind, error-based — with examples
  • XSS categories: Reflected XSS, stored XSS, DOM-based XSS — and prevention methods
  • Wireless attacks: KRACK, Evil Twin, Bluejacking/Bluesnarfing, WPA/WPA2 cracking with Aircrack-ng
  • Social engineering vectors: Phishing, vishing, smishing, tailgating, pretexting, baiting, quid pro quo

CEH vs Other Cybersecurity Certifications — Which Is Right for You?

Choosing the right cybersecurity certification can be confusing. Here's how CEH compares to other popular certifications:

CEH vs CompTIA Security+: CEH focuses on offensive security, penetration testing, and hands-on ethical hacking techniques while Security+ covers broader cybersecurity fundamentals including networking, cryptography, and governance. Security+ is often recommended as a first certification; CEV builds on that foundation with offensive skills. Choose CEH if you want to specialize in penetration testing.

CEH vs CISSP: CISSP is management-focused covering security architecture, policy development, risk management, and legal topics. It's ideal for senior professionals targeting management roles. CEH is hands-on technical ethical hacking perfect for penetration testers. Many professionals earn both: CISSP for management credibility and CEH for technical offensive skills.

CEH vs PenTest+: Both cover penetration testing but CEV has broader domain coverage including IoT/OT hacking, cloud computing security, mobile platform attacks, and cryptography. PenTest+ is more focused purely on penetration testing methodology. Choose CEV for broader career flexibility and PenTest+ for a more concise offensive security focus.

CEH Exam Cram — Last-Minute Study Plan

Exam tomorrow? Need to cram for the CEH exam? This last-minute CEV study plan covers only the highest-yield topics that account for the most exam points. Focus on these areas in order of priority:

  1. Port numbers (10 min) — Memorize: 21 FTP, 22 SSH, 23 Telnet, 25 SMTP, 53 DNS, 80 HTTP, 110 POP3, 143 IMAP, 443 HTTPS, 445 SMB, 993 IMAPS, 995 POP3S, 3306 MySQL, 3389 RDP, 8080 HTTP-Proxy
  2. Nmap scan types (15 min) — SYN stealth (-sS), TCP connect (-sT), FIN (-sF), XMAS (-sX), NULL (-sN), version detection (-sV), OS detection (-O), aggressive (-A), service/version (-sV), ping sweep (-sn)
  3. TCP three-way handshake (5 min) — SYN → SYN-ACK → ACK. Know how each Nmap scan interacts with it
  4. SQL injection categories (10 min) — Union-based (uses UNION SELECT), boolean-based blind (true/false responses), time-based blind (SLEEP/BENCHMARK), error-based (database errors revealed)
  5. XSS types (5 min) — Reflected (one-time via URL), stored (persisted in database), DOM-based (client-side only)
  6. Social engineering vectors (5 min) — Phishing (email), vishing (phone), smishing (SMS), pretexting (fabricated scenario), tailgating (physical entry), baiting (malicious USB)
  7. Cryptography basics (10 min) — AES (symmetric), RSA (asymmetric), SHA-256/MD5 (hash), digital signature (hash + private key encrypt), PKI (public key infrastructure)

Total cram time: ~45 minutes. These topics alone account for over 40% of all CEH exam questions. Use our free CEH flashcards to reinforce each category quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions About CEH Certification

How long does it take to prepare for the CEH exam?

Most candidates spend 4–8 weeks preparing, studying 10–15 hours per week using our free CEH study plan. If you already have security experience (Security+, CISSP, or hands-on penetration testing), you may need less time — as little as 2-3 weeks for a focused free CEH exam cram. Complete beginners should plan for the full 6–8 weeks.

Is CEH v13 harder than v12?

v13 introduces new topics like IoT/OT hacking, cloud computing security, and lateral movement while removing some legacy content. The overall difficulty is comparable but the exam now reflects more modern attack vectors. Focus on the new domains if you're switching from v12 to v13.

Can I pass CEH with just free flashcards?

Free CEH flashcards are excellent for memorizing key terms, port numbers, tool names, scan types, and attack vectors — but they're not sufficient alone. Combine them with hands-on labs (TryHackMe, HackTheBox), official study guides, practice exams, and our free CEV practice questions for the best chance of passing on your first attempt.

How much does the CEH exam cost?

The standalone exam voucher costs $1,199 USD. If you enroll in EC-Council's official training (CET), the exam is included for $849. Third-party vouchers may be available through authorized resellers at discounted rates. Our study materials — flashcards, practice questions, and study guide — are 100% free.
