CEH v13 Exam Format 2026: 125 Questions, 4 Hours, Passing Score

Last updated August 2026 · Everything you need to know before you book

Reviewed by Chris Pardue, CEH · Certified security practitioner · Last reviewed August 12, 2026

Before you spend a dollar on a voucher, you should know exactly what you are walking into. The EC-Council Certified Ethical Hacker v13 exam — code 312-50 — is a single, computer-based, multiple-choice sitting: 125 questions, 4 hours on the clock, and a passing line of 60% (75 correct). None of that is negotiable or hidden behind jargon. This page breaks down the full CEH v13 exam format for 2026 — the question types you will face, how the passing score actually works, what the voucher costs, and the step-by-step registration flow so nothing on test day is a surprise. Every number here matches the CEH v13 exam domains & blueprint on this site so your study plan and your expectations line up.

Question Types Explained

The "form-based" or computer-delivered nature of the CEH exam means every item is a multiple-choice question rendered on screen. There are exactly two formats you need to prepare for, and they require different answering habits:

Type 1: Single-answer multiple choice One correct option out of four (or five). This is the most common item type. The winning move is elimination — knock out the two clearly wrong answers first, then decide between the two that remain. On single-answer items there is no penalty for a considered guess, so never leave one blank.
Type 2: Multiple-answer (multiple response) The stem will say "select two" or "select all that apply," and you must pick every correct option — not too many, not too few. Treat each option independently as a true/false question instead of hunting for a theme. If three options are correct and you select only two, it is still marked wrong. This item type is where most points leak away, so slow down deliberately on any question that uses "all that apply" language.

What the exam is not matters just as much as what it is. There are no hands-on labs, no typed commands, no drag-and-drop reordering, and no essay or fill-in-the-blank items. You will never be asked to open a terminal and run Nmap; you will be asked what a SYN scan (-sS) does compared with a connect scan, or how to read its output. That means the exam tests conceptual fluency of tools like Nmap, scanning, and traffic analysis rather than your ability to operate them live — which is good news, because it makes free, self-directed prep genuinely sufficient. The same applies to web-attack concepts: you will reason about reflected versus stored XSS and SQL injection scenarios, not exploit a running target.

Passing Score: How It Actually Works

The passing threshold is 60%, which on a 125-question exam resolves to exactly 75 correct answers. A few details are worth internalizing before test day:

  • The result is immediate. When you submit, your pass or fail is displayed on the spot. There is no waiting days for a scaled score to arrive; you walk out knowing.
  • It is a hard line at 75. Seventy-four is a fail, seventy-five is a pass. That single-question gap is why a domain you skipped — even a 5% one worth about 6 questions — can be the difference between certified and retaking.
  • Blank questions are guaranteed misses. With no penalty for guessing on single-answer items, an unanswered question is the only sure way to lose a point you could have won.
  • If you miss, the 30-day rule applies. You can retake after 30 days, but only with a new voucher — the old one is consumed by your first attempt.

The practical implication for preparation: do not book at 60% confidence. A healthy booking threshold is 80%+ average on full-length, timed practice with no module below 60%. That buffer absorbs exam-day nerves so a normal bad stretch still leaves you safely above the line. You can build that measurement habit right now with the free 200-question practice bank — run a diagnostic across all 20 modules, record your raw score per domain, and use the numbers to decide when you are ready.

Time Budget & Pacing

Four hours for 125 questions is roughly 1 minute 53 seconds each, and the clock does not pause. The candidates who finish at 74/125 almost always lost more time than they lost knowledge — one hard question that ate five minutes cost them three easy ones they never reached.

The standard fix is a two-pass structure: on the first pass, answer anything you can do confidently in under 90 seconds and flag the rest; on the second pass, work only the flagged items with your remaining time. Reserve the final 10 minutes for review — no new answers, just confirming nothing is blank and re-checking your two hardest guesses. The How to Pass CEH v13 strategy expands this into a full exam-day playbook, including reading the last line of long questions first, since that line contains the actual ask.

How to Register (Step by Step)

Registration has four steps and takes about 30 minutes total, plus your study timeline. The full walkthrough with screenshots and ID requirements lives in the CEH exam registration guide; here is the spine:

1. Create an EC-Council account A free member account on the EC-Council website is required to buy a voucher and track your certification status. Have a valid email address ready.
2. Purchase the exam voucher Buy the CEH v13 (312-50) voucher through EC-Council at the member or non-member price. Note your voucher code and the 6-month expiry date on your calendar.
3. Schedule with Pearson VUE Enter your voucher code on Pearson VUE and pick either a test center near you or online proctoring. Choose a date after you have met your 80%+ booking threshold, not before.
4. Prepare for check-in Online proctored: run the system check the day before, confirm your ID matches your profile exactly, and clear your desk and camera view. Test center: arrive 30 minutes early with a government-issued ID.

Half of every "exam day disaster" is administrative — an ID mismatch, an unrun system check, or walking into a center late. Clear all four steps in advance and test day becomes exactly what it should be: 125 questions you have already practiced 20 times over.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many questions are on the CEH v13 exam?

The CEH v13 (312-50) exam contains 125 multiple-choice questions. All 20 exam modules/domains are represented, so even the lowest-weighted domains can appear — a domain at 5% is still about 6 questions.

How long is the CEH v13 exam?

You get 4 hours (240 minutes) to complete all 125 questions, which works out to roughly 1 minute and 53 seconds per question. The clock runs continuously for the whole sitting; there is no separate unscored preview block you should spend time on.

What is the CEH exam passing score?

The passing score is 60%, which equals 75 correct answers out of 125. Your pass or fail result is shown immediately at the end of the exam. If you do not pass, you can retake it after 30 days with a new voucher.

What types of questions are on the CEH exam?

The exam is fully computer-based and multiple-choice. It includes single-answer questions (pick one option) and multiple-answer questions (select two or more options). There are no hands-on labs, no typed commands, no drag-and-drop, and no essay items — every question has fixed lettered choices.

How much does the CEH v13 exam cost?

The exam voucher is $1,250 USD for EC-Council members and $1,450 for non-members. The voucher is valid for 6 months from purchase and covers one attempt. Rescheduling through Pearson VUE is typically around $95, and a failed attempt requires buying a new voucher.

Can I take the CEH exam online or only at a test center?

Both. The CEH v13 exam is delivered by Pearson VUE either at a physical test center or as an online proctored exam from home. Both formats contain the same 125 questions and the same 4-hour limit; online proctoring requires a system check, a quiet room, stable internet, and a government-issued ID.
