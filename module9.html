Module 9: Social Engineering

Section 1: Social Engineering Fundamentals

Social engineering = the art of manipulating people to perform actions or divulge confidential information. It exploits human psychology rather than technical vulnerabilities.

The "CIA" of social engineering:

  • Credibility: The target believes the attacker is legitimate
  • Conviction: The attacker shows confidence and determination
  • Capacity: The attacker has the ability to carry out the request

Information Gathering Phases

  1. Reconnaissance: Gather information about the target (website, social media, LinkedIn)
  2. Building trust and rapport: Establish credibility with the target
  3. Maintaining the cover story: Keep the ruse going during interaction
  4. Exploitation: Get what you want (credentials, access, information)

Psychological Triggers Used in Social Engineering

  • Authority: Impersonate someone in power (CEO, IT admin, law enforcement)
  • Urgency: Create time pressure to prevent critical thinking
  • Fear: Threaten negative consequences if action not taken
  • Greed: Offer something too good to be true
  • Helpfulness: Exploit people's desire to be helpful
  • Familiarity/Liking: Build rapport and common ground

Section 2: Social Engineering Techniques

Phishing

Fraudulent attempts to obtain sensitive information by disguising as a trustworthy entity in electronic communication. Usually sent in bulk to many recipients.

Phishing Methods

  • Email phishing: Fake emails from legitimate companies asking for credentials or personal info
  • Link redirection: Links redirect to fake websites that look identical to real ones
  • Drive-by downloads: Links/downloads that automatically install malware
  • Spear phishing: Targeted phishing aimed at specific individuals or organizations

Whaling

Phishing attack targeting high-level executives (CEO, CFO, CTO). More sophisticated and personalized than regular phishing.

Pharming

Redirecting users from legitimate websites to fake ones. Can be done by:

  • Exploiting vulnerabilities in DNS servers (DNS poisoning)
  • Modifying hosts files on target machines
  • Attacking DHCP servers to provide wrong DNS settings

Spear Phishing

Personalized phishing attack targeting specific individuals. Uses personal information (name, job title, colleagues) to appear more credible.

Phases

  1. Reconnaissance: Gather info about the target from social media, LinkedIn, company website
  2. Personalized message: Craft a message that appears relevant to the target
  3. Exploitation: Trick target into revealing credentials or clicking malicious links

Vishing (Voice Phishing)

Phishing via telephone calls. Attacker poses as a representative of a legitimate company (bank, IT support, government agency) to extract sensitive information.

Tailgating (Piggybacking)

Physical social engineering where attacker follows authorized person into a restricted area without proper credentials. E.g., holding the door open while carrying items.

Countermeasures

  • Challenge tailgaters and ask for identification
  • Install mantraps (two-door entry systems)
  • Implement strict badge policies

Impersonation

Attacker pretends to be someone the target trusts — IT support, coworker, vendor, government official. Can be done in person, over phone, or via email.

Troll Attack

Three-party social engineering attack:

  1. Troll (attacker): Calls the victim pretending to be from another department
  2. Mole (accomplice): Works in the target organization and provides information
  3. Mark (victim): The target of the attack

Diversion Theft

Redirecting legitimate deliveries or shipments to the attacker. E.g., intercepting a package containing credentials or hardware tokens.

Pretexting

Creating a fabricated scenario (pretext) to manipulate the target into divulging information. The attacker develops an elaborate story and plays a role.

Example

Attacker calls IT support claiming they locked out of their account and need their password reset. They provide personal information gathered from reconnaissance to appear legitimate.

Baiting

Offering something free or enticing to the victim (e.g., free movie download, USB drive left in parking lot). When the victim interacts with it, malware is installed or data is stolen.

Example

  • USB drive labeled "Executive Salaries" left in a public area
  • Free software download that contains keyloggers
  • "You've won a prize!" pop-up ads

Watering Hole

Compromising websites frequently visited by the target audience. Instead of targeting individuals directly, attackers infect sites those individuals routinely visit.

  1. Identify websites regularly visited by target group
  2. Inject malware into those websites
  3. When targets visit the site, malware is delivered

Denial of Service as Social Engineering

Triggering a DoS attack on a company's systems to create urgency for a subsequent social engineering attack (e.g., offering emergency IT support during the outage).

Cybergrubbing

Gathering information from social media platforms, forums, and online communities to build profiles of targets for more targeted attacks.

Cloning

Replicating a mobile phone's IMSI and MIN/MRN numbers to hijack calls and texts. Used to intercept 2FA codes.

Section 3: Social Engineering Tools

Social-Engineer Toolkit (SET)

Open-source penetration testing framework by TrustedSec. Designed for social engineering attacks.

Features

  • Website Attack Vectors: Clone websites, create fake login pages
  • Email Attack Vectors: Craft phishing emails with tracking
  • Clipboard Attacks: Modify Flash/HTML clipboard content
  • Binary/Exploit Attacks: Generate payloads
  • Multimedia Attacks: Encode payloads into AVI/MP3/WAV files
  • Credential Harvesting: Capture credentials from fake login pages

SET Commands

# Run SET
sudo setoolkit

# Select social-engine attacks
1. Social-Engineering Attacks
2. Website Attack Vectors
3. Credential Harvester Attack Method
4. Select site to clone and harvest credentials
5. Enter target URL to clone
6. Execute exploit

Gophish

Open-source phishing framework. Features include:

  • Email campaign management
  • Landing page creation
  • SMTP server configuration
  • Results tracking and reporting

Maltego

Intelligence gathering tool for visualizing relationships between people, groups, pages, domains, IPs, etc. Used in reconnaissance phase of social engineering.

TheHarvester

Gathers emails, subdomains, hosts, employee names, and open-port information from public sources (search engines, PGP key servers).

Recon-ng

Full-featured web reconnaissance framework with a modular interface and working environment similar to Ruby's Metasploit.

Malware Development Tools

  • MSFVenom: Generate payloads for social engineering attacks
  • Mimikatz: Extract credentials from memory
  • Empire: PowerShell-based post-exploitation framework

Section 4: Social Engineering Countermeasures

Awareness Training

  • Regular security awareness training for all employees
  • Social engineering simulation exercises (phishing tests)
  • Educate about common attack techniques and red flags
  • Train on proper procedures for handling sensitive data

Technical Controls

  • Email filtering and spam protection
  • Multi-factor authentication (MFA)
  • Web application firewalls (WAF)
  • DNS security extensions (DNSSEC)
  • Domain-based Message Authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC)

Physical Security

  • Badge access systems
  • CCTV surveillance
  • Mantraps and security guards
  • Clear desk and clean screen policies
  • Proper disposal of sensitive documents (shredding)

Policies and Procedures

  • Establish verification procedures for sensitive requests
  • Create incident response plans for social engineering attacks
  • Implement least privilege access controls
  • Document and communicate acceptable use policies

Red Flags of Social Engineering

  • Unsolicited calls/emails asking for sensitive information
  • Urgency or pressure to act immediately
  • Inconsistencies in emails (sender address, grammar)
  • Requests that bypass normal procedures
  • Unexpected USB drives or packages
  • People who don't need badges in secure areas

Section 5: Notable Social Engineering Incidents

BBC Newsroom Attack (2017)

Attacker posed as an IT support worker over the phone, tricking staff into revealing credentials. This led to a ransomware attack that encrypted files and disrupted news production.

Dunbar Security Breach (2018)

Attacker called Dunbar's HR department pretending to be an employee needing to change direct deposit information. Provided personal details gathered from reconnaissance to appear legitimate.

Burger King UK Social Engineering (2016)

Attackers called Burger King UK locations, posing as corporate staff and tricking managers into revealing system credentials by threatening immediate termination if they didn't comply.

Morgan Stanley Attack (2012)

Attacker posed as a Google engineer to gain access to Morgan Stanley's data center. Used social engineering combined with physical intrusion techniques.
